Systems & funnel blueprints

Five builds, mapped end to end.

Each blueprint shows the steps a person goes through, the system behind each step, how the journey branches and what I check before it goes live. These are representative builds, not diagrams of a specific client's account.

Representative blueprints
Blueprint 01

Assessment funnel: questions → personalised results → CRM → email

A personality profile, readiness check or business diagnostic that captures answers, classifies an outcome and sends each person down the follow-up that matches their result.

Landing pageAssessment toolCRMEmail automationCalendar

Journey steps

  1. TrafficTraffic or referral
  2. PageAssessment landing page
  3. FormAssessment questions
  4. FormLead capture: name and email
  5. CRMAnswers mapped to CRM fields
  6. LogicScoring or outcome classification
  7. PagePersonalised results page
  8. CRMOutcome tags and segment
  9. EmailResults delivery email
  10. EmailOutcome-specific nurture
  11. CalendarRelevant offer or booking CTA
  12. CRMFollow-up and lead tracking

Branches

Outcome assigned

Outcome A

Result A page and email, nurture A, CTA matched to A.

Outcome B

Result B page and email, nurture B, CTA matched to B.

Outcome C

Result C page and email, nurture C, CTA matched to C.

Started, not finished

Partial-completion reminder, only if contact details were captured first.

Operational logic

  • Capture contact details and every answer.
  • Map each answer to its own CRM field.
  • Assign the outcome using the assessment's rules.
  • Apply the matching outcome tag or segment.
  • Show or send the correct result.
  • Trigger the matching sequence only once (guard against duplicate enrolment).
  • Exit the nurture when the person books or buys.

What I test before launch

  • Complete the assessment once per outcome with a test contact.
  • Check fields, tags and workflow enrolment on each test record.
  • Retake with the same email: confirm no duplicate contact or second sequence.
  • Check every result link and booking link.

Automatic scoring calculates the outcome instantly. Manual review holds the result until a person checks the answers, then a tag or field change releases the right email. They're built differently, so I confirm which one the business uses first.

Blueprint 02

Masterclass funnel: registration → reminders → live event → replay → offer

From promotion to replay: registration, reminders in the right time zone, attendance-based follow-up and a clear next step after the event.

Event pageRegistration formCRMEmail automationZoom or event platform

Journey steps

  1. PagePromotional landing page
  2. FormRegistration form
  3. CRMContact created or updated
  4. CRMEvent registration tag
  5. EmailConfirmation with joining details
  6. CalendarAdd-to-calendar link
  7. EmailReminder sequence
  8. EventLive event
  9. CRMAttendance classification, where available
  10. EmailReplay email
  11. EmailFollow-up nurture
  12. CalendarOffer or booking CTA

Branches

After the event

Attended

Thank-you, key takeaways and the offer while it's fresh.

Registered, didn't attend

Replay email with a deadline; reminders to watch.

Attended, no next step

Shorter nurture answering common objections.

Clicked offer or booked

Leaves the event follow-up and moves to sales or onboarding, where tracking is available.

Operational logic

  • Event date and time stored once and reused, so changes update everywhere.
  • Reminders timed from the event, displayed in the registrant's time zone where the tools allow.
  • Registration tag per event, so past registrants aren't messaged about the wrong session.
  • Attendance data imported or synced after the event, where the platform provides it.
  • Replay access closed or redirected after the deadline.

What I test before launch

  • Register test contacts in different time zones.
  • Check each reminder's send time and joining link.
  • Simulate attended and not-attended contacts after the event.
  • Confirm buyers stop receiving the offer emails.

If the event platform doesn't report attendance, everyone receives the replay version. I'd say so plainly rather than pretend the follow-up is more personalised than it is.

Blueprint 03

Book-a-call funnel: landing page → VSL → booked call → follow-up

A sales page with a video sales letter that moves interested people to a booked call, with the CRM and reminders handling everything around the appointment.

Landing pageVideoBooking calendarCRM / pipelineEmail & SMS reminders

Journey steps

  1. TrafficTraffic or referral
  2. PageSales landing page
  3. PageVideo sales letter
  4. PageCTA: book a call
  5. CalendarBooking calendar with intake questions
  6. EmailBooking confirmation
  7. CRMContact and opportunity created
  8. EmailAppointment reminders
  9. EmailPre-call nurture
  10. CalendarCall: attended, cancelled or no-show
  11. EmailOutcome-specific follow-up
  12. CRMPipeline stage updated

Branches

After the call time

Attended

Notes logged, opportunity moves to the next stage, post-call follow-up starts.

No-show

No-show email with a rebook link; opportunity flagged for the closer.

Cancelled

Reschedule invitation; reminders for the old time are stopped.

Rescheduled

Reminders reset to the new time; one record, no duplicates.

Operational logic

  • Calendar connected to the CRM so a booking creates or updates one contact.
  • Lead source and intake answers saved to fields.
  • Confirmation sent immediately; reminders timed relative to the appointment.
  • Internal notification to whoever takes the call.
  • Pipeline stage changes on booking, attendance and outcome, where a pipeline is configured.

What I test before launch

  • Book, reschedule and cancel with a test contact.
  • Check reminder timing in two different time zones.
  • Mark a test call as no-show and confirm the right sequence starts.
  • Confirm the closer receives the notification with the intake answers.

This journey is where marketing hands over to sales. The aim is that the person taking the call already has the context, and nobody falls through the gap between booked and attended.

Blueprint 04

Application funnel: multi-step application → review → qualified call

For high-ticket offers: a multi-step application that captures the right information, routes each applicant appropriately and only invites qualified people to book.

Application pageMulti-step formCRMEmailBooking calendar

Journey steps

  1. PageApplication landing page
  2. FormMulti-step application
  3. CRMContact and application data captured
  4. CRMSegment by answers
  5. LogicQualification rules or manual review
  6. EmailInternal notification
  7. EmailBooking invitation for qualified leads
  8. CalendarBooking confirmation and reminders
  9. CalendarSales conversation
  10. CRMFollow-up and pipeline progress

Branches

After the application

Qualified

Invitation to book, with confirmation and reminders.

Not a fit right now

Kind decline, plus a lower-commitment resource or offer.

Incomplete

Reminder to finish, if contact details were captured on step one.

Needs review

Held for a person to decide; status field updated when they do.

Operational logic

  • Capture contact details on the first step so incomplete applications can be followed up.
  • Store each answer in its own field for review and reporting.
  • Automatic qualification only where clear rules have been agreed; otherwise manual review.
  • Booking link sent only to qualified applicants.
  • Status field records the decision, so reporting stays accurate.

What I test before launch

  • Submit one qualified, one unqualified and one incomplete test application.
  • Confirm each lands in the right segment and gets the right email.
  • Check the reviewer's notification includes the answers.

I don't assume automated qualification. Many businesses review applications by hand, and the system should support that rather than guess.

Blueprint 05

Lead gathering funnel: opt-in → CRM → nurture → conversion

A free resource that brings people in, delivers what was promised immediately, then nurtures them toward one clear next step, and stops when they take it.

Opt-in pageFormCRMEmail automation

Journey steps

  1. PageLead magnet landing page
  2. FormOpt-in form
  3. CRMContact captured
  4. CRMSource and interest tags
  5. EmailAsset delivery
  6. EmailWelcome sequence
  7. EmailEducational nurture
  8. CRMEngagement-based segment, where configured
  9. PageRelevant offer
  10. CalendarBook, apply or buy

Branches

During the nurture

Converts

Exits immediately; moves to the booking, application or customer journey.

Engaged, not converted

Continues nurture; may get a more direct offer.

Not engaging

Moves to a lighter re-engagement path, or is suppressed.

Operational logic

  • Deliver the asset instantly, with a backup link in case the email is filtered.
  • Tag the source and the topic of interest.
  • Goal or exit condition on every nurture: booked, applied or purchased.
  • Suppress contacts already in a sales or customer journey.
  • Avoid re-sending the welcome sequence when someone opts in a second time.

What I test before launch

  • Opt in with a new email and an existing one.
  • Check the delivery email and the download link.
  • Trigger the conversion action and confirm the nurture stops.

The most common gap I see here is the missing exit: people keep receiving 'ready to start?' emails after they've already bought.
